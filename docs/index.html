Do banks that talk more about the environment earn less?

We re-ran the analysis of a published study on ten large Indian banks. Here is what the data say, and what they cannot say.

The study in one minute

The question
Pawar & Munuswamy (2024) ask whether banks that publish more information about their environmental activities are more or less profitable.
What they found
Banks with more environmental reporting had lower profits, measured by return on assets (ROA) and return on equity (ROE). Earnings per share showed no clear link.
What we found
We reproduced their estimates of the main effects exactly, using their public data. But the negative link only appears once other bank characteristics, above all bad loans, are held constant. On their own, the raw numbers point the other way.
The catch
This is a correlation, not proof of cause. With banks over years, the evidence is too thin to say that reporting makes banks less profitable.

So does reporting cost banks money?

  • Bad loans drive the flip. Banks with more bad loans report less and earn less. Ignore bad loans, and the reporting score looks good because it stands in for "healthy bank".
  • Correlation, not cause. Better or worse managed banks may both report differently and earn differently. And profits might drive reporting, not the other way round.
  • Small sample. Ten banks over five years leave little room. Most of the differences in reporting are between banks, not changes within a bank over time.
